---
title: 'Precision Bass Conversion'
pubDate: 2026-10-04
description: 'how I converted my Squier Precision Bass into a custom P-MM hybrid'
---
This was one of my most difficult projects to date. It started out as a Squier Affinity PJ
bass.

It now has one P-style pickup, and one MM-style in the bridge, which is a humbucking (dual coil) pickup. The MM is both narrower and taller than the original J pickup, which required a new cavity to be cut.

The bass now has five knobs instead of three, with two toggle switches added on the lower horn as well. All of its
electronics are still passive.

The toggle switches control how the coils in the bridge-position are wired. This is done by having a DPDT 2 position switch either connect the two coils in series or parallel, and the second DPDT 3 position switch shorting out one coil or the other when pushed to one side or the other.

The first knob is a master volume control foknob, using ar both pickups. The second is a special potentiometer. In the middle position, both pickups are at full volume, and turning it to one side or the other reduces the volume of one pickup. The third knob is a master high-pass filter, and the last two knobs are low-pass filters, one for each pickup.

The body needed the pickup cavity and lower horn cavity cut out, as well as some additional space in the control cavity, and a wiring channel to the switch cavity. I designed the cavities in CAD, and 3D printed routing templates to get the perfect fit for the pickup, and a clean, properly sized switch cavity and wiring channel.

The majority of cutting was with three different lengths of cheap 1/2" straight cut top bearing bits, with 1/8 to 3/16 depth of cut each pass, and the corners were done with an Amana Tool top-bearing 1/4" diameter, 3/4" length router bit, which left the perfect radius for the corners of the pickup.
The only router available to me for this was a mid-size plunge router, which worked well for the pickup cavity, but was quite inconvenient for the smaller areas, I've since gotten a trim router that I would've preferred for this project. There was a chunk taken out of the edge of the switch cavity, but it wasn't a serious problem.

Wiring took by far the longest out of any part of the project other than planning.The solder I was using was on the old side, so I struggled at some points to make good joints.
I used three connectors to allow the pickups, output jack, and bridge grounding wire to be separated from the other wiring, so that the pickguard could be fully removed from the body.

The middle potentiometer was originally meant for the mid-cut circuit in the vintage P pickup I was gifted, but it failed to work as intended, so I chose to use a capacitor I had laying around to turn it into a high-pass filter. I also replaced the bridge with a heavier one that improved playability.

I did have to take it apart again twice to get every part of the wiring working properly,
but a large part of that was due to being unable to find sufficient documentation on the vintage SGD bridge pickup, which had an unusual color code for the wires.

Overall the project came out very well, and the sound and character was improved in the way I was looking for. The bass is currently in need of some professional work on the neck, but
I've spent many hours playing the bass since I finished the modifications,  and I am quite pleased with it.
